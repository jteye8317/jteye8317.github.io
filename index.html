About

Empirical economics for questions that matter.

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at West Virginia University, expected to graduate in May 2027. My primary research fields are health economics and labor economics, with secondary interests in public economics, applied microeconometrics, political economy, and urban and regional economics.

I am especially interested in how public programs affect insurance coverage, access, work, and the administrative experience of households—and in translating rigorous empirical work into clear evidence for policy and teaching.

Job Market Paper 2026

Extending Postpartum Medicaid Without Discouraging Work

The American Rescue Plan Act allowed states to extend pregnancy-related Medicaid coverage from sixty days to twelve months postpartum. Using American Community Survey data and a triple-differences design, I study how the policy affected insurance coverage and labor-market outcomes.

Main finding

The extension increased Medicaid coverage among low-income recent mothers by roughly 3–4 percentage points, with no systematic reduction in employment, labor-force participation, or hours worked.

Teaching

Teaching economics as a way of thinking with evidence.

Download full philosophy ↗

I teach economics the way I do research: by starting from a real question, reasoning carefully from evidence, and being honest about what the data can and cannot tell us.

My goal is for students to move beyond memorizing formulas and develop the confidence to ask meaningful questions, analyze data, and evaluate empirical claims critically.

01

Research-Driven Teaching

I anchor statistical and econometric ideas in questions students can feel the weight of—such as how a Medicaid policy change affects whether new mothers keep health coverage, or how we separate a policy effect from everything else happening at the same time.

02

Teaching by Doing

I solve problems step by step, narrate the reasoning behind each move, and use “try-first” questions, board work, and active problem solving so students learn by practicing rather than only watching.

03

Analytical & Research Skills

I want students to leave able to clean and interrogate data, choose an appropriate method, and evaluate an empirical argument on its merits using tools such as R, Stata, and Python.

04

Structure, Rigor & Inclusion

I use complete notes, linked explanatory videos, frequent low-stakes practice, and review so students encounter difficulty while there is still time to fix it—without lowering standards.
