About Me

Research at the intersection of health, labor, and public policy.

My primary fields are health economics and labor economics. My broader work spans public economics, applied microeconometrics, political economy, and urban and regional economics. I use causal-inference methods and large administrative and survey datasets to study policy questions with direct implications for households and public programs.

My dissertation, Three Essays on Public Policy: Health Insurance, Administrative Burden, and Legislative Behavior, is chaired by Professor Daniel Grossman.

Featured Job Market Paper

Extending Postpartum Medicaid Without Discouraging Work

Evidence from the 12-Month Coverage Extension

Using American Community Survey data and a triple-differences design, I study the expansion of pregnancy-related Medicaid coverage from sixty days to twelve months postpartum.

The extension increased Medicaid coverage among low-income recent mothers by roughly 3–4 percentage points, with no systematic reduction in employment, labor-force participation, or hours worked.
