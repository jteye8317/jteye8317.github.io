Teaching Philosophy

Helping students learn to reason with evidence.

Many students arrive convinced that they are “not math people.” My goal is to show that, with clear structure and the right kind of practice, students can learn to think like empirical economists: to read quantitative claims critically, reason about evidence, and communicate conclusions clearly.

Teaching Approach

Research-driven, active, and structured.

Research-Driven Teaching

I connect statistical and econometric ideas to substantive policy questions and show how empirical methods help answer questions that matter.

Teaching by Doing

I work through problems step by step, narrate the reasoning behind each move, and use active problem solving rather than relying only on lecture.

Analytical & Research Skills

I want students to leave able to clean and interrogate data, choose an appropriate method, and evaluate empirical arguments on their merits.

Structure, Rigor & Inclusion

I use organized materials, frequent low-stakes practice, and timely feedback to support students while maintaining rigorous standards.
